6. ROOT-CAUSE DIAGNOSTICS & PERFORMANCE DIVERGENCE ANALYSIS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
While the calibrated SLA model achieved an internal holdout ROC-AUC of 0.8840 and
PR-AUC of 0.9200 on 362 unseen historical tickets from the original operational
distribution, its discrimination on this synthetic external test dataset is
substantially lower (ROC-AUC 0.5573, PR-AUC 0.5441). Diagnostic decomposition
reveals the exact reasons for this behavior:

1. Severe Category Covariate Shift:
   In the operational baseline dataset, ticket category was the single strongest
   predictor of SLA breach risk:
     * Payments & Checkout: 89.28% historical breach rate
     * API Integrations:    77.80% historical breach rate
     * Fulfilment:          59.87% historical breach rate
     * Account Access:      24.44% historical breach rate
     * Notifications:       18.10% historical breach rate
   In this synthetic external test set, the empirical breach rates across categories
   have been flattened to ~50%:
     * Payments & Checkout: 50.00% breach rate (shift: -39.28 pp)
     * Notifications:       50.52% breach rate (shift: +32.42 pp)
     * API Integrations:    50.96% breach rate (shift: -26.84 pp)
     * Fulfilment:          52.48% breach rate (shift:  -7.39 pp)
     * Account Access:      31.82% breach rate (shift:  +7.38 pp)
   Because the Random Forest classifier assigns high feature importance to category
   one-hot columns (e.g. Payments & Checkout: 0.1773, Notifications: 0.1630), removing
   the real-world category breach risk gradient eliminates the primary predictive signal.

2. Out-of-Vocabulary Subcategory Shift (44.0% of Test Dataset):
   220 of the 500 test tickets feature subcategories never encountered during training
   (e.g., "Account Lockout", "Login Failure", "MFA Issue", "Email Not Received",
   "Push Delivery Failure", "SMS Delay", "Template Error", "Tracking Update Delay",
   "Order Status Error"). Under strict adherence to zero-leakage and zero-guesswork
   rules, no manual mapping was fabricated; the pipeline's OneHotEncoder(handle_unknown="ignore")
   correctly assigned zeros across all subcategory dummy columns for these tickets,
   depriving the ensemble of secondary diagnostic features.

3. Compression of Priority Risk Gradient:
   In training, P1 and P2 tickets suffered acute breach rates (71.88% and 74.58%,
   respectively), whereas P4 tickets had a 50.11% breach rate. In the synthetic test
   set, P2 breaches drop sharply to 51.81%, and P4 breaches drop to 36.52%.
